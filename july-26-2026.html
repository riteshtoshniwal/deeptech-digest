Deep Tech Startups & Investments

Manufacturing and DeepTech Overtake AI in India's $209 Mn Funding Week

Indian startups raised $209 Mn in the week ending 25 July 2026 — a 44% drop from the prior week's $346 Mn — but capital rotated decisively from AI toward manufacturing, aerospace, enterprise software and healthtech. Deal count fell to 14 rounds, and July's monthly total stands at roughly $820 Mn versus $1.91 Bn in June. (Published: 2026-07-25)

Zetwerk Raises $52 Mn Pre-IPO Round, the Week's Largest Deal

Manufacturing platform Zetwerk secured $52 Mn in a pre-IPO round, the largest Indian startup deal reported in the week ending 25 July. The raise reflects investor confidence in tech-enabled industrial supply chain businesses ahead of a public listing. (Reported in weekly roundup published: 2026-07-25)
- TICE News

Raghu Vamsi Aerospace Secures $40 Mn for Defence Manufacturing

Hyderabad-based Raghu Vamsi Aerospace raised $40 Mn from Norwest Venture Partners, Skegen Asset Management and investor Ashish Kacholia — a marker of growing investor appetite for aerospace and defence manufacturing under India's advanced-manufacturing policy push. (Reported in weekly roundup published: 2026-07-25)

BusinessNext, Plazza, CARPL and Bioscan Round Out the Week's Deals

Banking-tech firm BusinessNext raised a $40 Mn Series C led entirely by ServiceNow Ventures; digital health startup Plazza secured $15 Mn from Accel, Elevation Capital and Nexus Venture Partners; healthcare AI platform CARPL raised $10 Mn from IFC and Stellaris; and Bioscan Research led seed activity with $1 Mn from Unicorn India Ventures. (Reported in weekly roundup published: 2026-07-25)

AI & Machine Learning

Venture Capital Rotates Away From AI Mega-Rounds in India

After AI dominated the previous week's $346 Mn haul, the week ending 25 July saw no AI mega-deals at all — investors instead backed factories, banking software, aerospace and healthcare infrastructure. Analysts read this as selectivity rather than retreat: capital is converging on sectors aligned with India's long-term industrial priorities, with mature AI startups increasingly leaning on insider-led and bridge rounds while awaiting better IPO conditions. (Published: 2026-07-25)

Regional Tech Policy & Regulations

100% FDI Approved for Inventory-Based E-Commerce Exports

The Government of India approved 100% Foreign Direct Investment in inventory-based e-commerce models for exports during the week, a move expected to widen global market access for Indian manufacturers, exporters and consumer brands — and open new funding avenues for export-facing startups. (Reported in weekly roundup published: 2026-07-25)

The Centre's revamped ₹10,000-crore Startup India Fund of Funds 2.0 consolidates public funding mechanisms and steers capital toward deeptech, manufacturing and Tier-2/Tier-3 startups — converging government strategy with the week's private funding trends. Key Takeaways

  1. Capital is rotating, not retreating. India's $209 Mn week was 44% down on the prior week, but the mix shifted from AI mega-rounds to manufacturing (Zetwerk), aerospace/defence (Raghu Vamsi), enterprise software (BusinessNext) and healthtech (Plazza, CARPL) — a diversification story rather than a slowdown story.

  2. Policy and VC are converging on deeptech. The 100% FDI approval for inventory-based e-commerce exports and the ₹10,000 Cr Fund of Funds 2.0 both point the same direction as private capital: manufacturing, deeptech and beyond-metro startups.

  3. Seed remains tight. Just ~$1.8 Mn went into three seed deals — early-stage investors continue to demand proof of product-market fit before first cheques.

  4. Weekend news cycle was light. 25 July was a Saturday; no confirmed 25-July-dated breach disclosures, AI launches or SEA funding announcements surfaced in date-filtered searches.
