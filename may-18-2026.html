🇮🇳 India AI & Market Analysis

India Misses Out on AI Boom — Market Darling Status at Risk

India stands out as one of the biggest losers as the artificial intelligence trade reshapes global investment flows. The country's stock market is on the verge of dropping out of the world's five biggest for the first time in three years, as foreign investors have pulled $21 billion from Indian equities in just two months. Global capital is chasing AI themes that India's market largely lacks: chip manufacturing, computing infrastructure, and frontier AI models. India's MSCI index weight has fallen from 19% to about 12% over the past year, with roughly two-thirds of that reallocation reflecting AI positioning by global funds. Without AI-driven rallies powering markets like Taiwan and South Korea, analysts warn India faces a structural gap rather than a cyclical dip. (Published: 2026-05-17)

🛡️ Cybersecurity

Silver Fox Deploys ABCDoor Malware via Tax-Themed Phishing in India, Indonesia & Russia

China-linked threat actor Silver Fox (tracked as UTG-Q-1000) launched a new campaign targeting organisations in India, Indonesia, Russia, South Africa and Japan with a previously undocumented Python-based backdoor called ABCDoor. The campaign used socially engineered phishing emails impersonating India's Income Tax Department to deliver the ABCDoor payload alongside ValleyRAT via modified Rust loaders. ABCDoor enables persistent remote access, screenshot capture, keylogging, clipboard exfiltration, and file system operations. Kaspersky, which first detected waves since December 2025, identified more than 1,600 malicious messages sent to industrial, consulting, retail, and transportation sector targets. India had the highest volume of targeted attacks. (Published: 2026-05-04)

Mustang Panda LOTUSLITE v1.1 Backdoor Targets India's Banking Sector

China-linked APT group Mustang Panda has expanded its cyber espionage operations to India's financial sector, deploying the updated LOTUSLITE v1.1 backdoor. Beginning in March 2026, attackers distributed malicious CHM files disguised as banking documents (e.g., "Request for Support.chm") to employees at Indian banks, using fake HDFC Bank pop-ups to facilitate drive-by JavaScript downloads. The campaign also simultaneously targeted political circles in South Korea. LOTUSLITE enables deep system surveillance with persistent access. (Published: 2026-04)

🤖 AI Policy & Regulation

India's 2026 AI Governance Guidelines & IT Amendment Rules

India's Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY) formally notified the 2026 IT Amendment Rules on February 20, 2026, mandating AI content labeling and reducing harmful content takedown times to 3 hours for large online platforms. Separately, at the India AI Impact Summit, the government outlined an AI Governance Framework covering deployment, accountability, and safety. Global firms operating in India now face legal compliance risks under the new 2026 AI regulation framework. (Published: 2026)

Tech executives from India's AI infrastructure sector told the Gitex AI Asia 2026 conference in Singapore that India's massive scale will serve as a testing ground for the broader Asian market. 💡 Key Takeaways & Insights

1. India's AI investment gap is real and widening. The Bloomberg analysis published on May 17 captures a structural concern: global capital is reallocating toward AI-native hardware and model infrastructure, and India's equity markets lack these assets. Without urgent moves in chip manufacturing and frontier AI, the market headwinds are likely to persist.

2. China-linked cyber threats intensify across India and SEA. Both Silver Fox (ABCDoor campaign) and Mustang Panda (LOTUSLITE v1.1) are actively running targeted espionage operations against Indian enterprises — particularly banking and industrial sectors. Indonesian organisations are also Silver Fox targets. Cybersecurity teams should urgently review phishing defences and monitor for anomalous CHM file usage.

3. Deep tech hardware is India's funding bright spot. AI chip startups like HrdWyr are attracting institutional capital, and semiconductor deals dominated the May 13 funding week. This suggests India's deep tech ecosystem is beginning to develop indigenous hardware capability — a potential long-term response to the AI investment gap.

4. Southeast Asia's cybersecurity market is booming. With the APAC cybersecurity market worth $83.9B in 2026 and growing at 13.5% CAGR, vendors like NinjaOne are doubling down on the region. Indonesia, Malaysia, Philippines and India are all seeing accelerating demand for managed security services.

5. India's AI governance framework is taking shape. The 2026 IT Amendment Rules and AI Governance Guidelines signal India's intent to be an active regulator of AI — not just a consumer. Foreign tech platforms operating in India must now budget for compliance costs around AI content labeling and expedited takedown obligations.
