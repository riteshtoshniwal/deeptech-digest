Cybersecurity

"Agentjacking" — AI coding agents hijacked to run attacker code

Researchers disclosed a new class of attack dubbed "Agentjacking" that hijacks AI coding agents and silently executes attacker-controlled code on developer machines using a single injected Sentry configuration. The technique highlights the expanding attack surface created by autonomous AI developer tooling. (Published: 2026-06-13)
- The Hacker News
- The Hacker News — Vulnerability

Critical RCE chain disclosed in Splunk Enterprise

A critical vulnerability chain in Splunk Enterprise was disclosed that lets unauthenticated attackers achieve remote code execution via a misconfigured PostgreSQL sidecar. Enterprises running self-managed Splunk deployments are advised to review configurations and apply mitigations. (Published: 2026-06-13)
- The Hacker News

LangGraph AI-agent framework vulnerability chain

A critical vulnerability chain was reported in LangGraph, the popular open-source AI-agent framework from the creators of LangChain, which could allow attackers to gain unauthorized access. The disclosure adds to a growing run of security issues in the AI-agent toolchain. (Published: 2026-06-13)
- The Hacker News — Vulnerability

BugHunter — open-source bug-bounty toolkit built on Claude Code

A new open-source bug-bounty hunting toolkit called BugHunter was released, built on top of Anthropic's Claude Code and extended to support free AI providers. It signals continued momentum in AI-assisted offensive-security tooling. (Published: 2026-06-13)
- The Hacker News

GPS Renewables raises ₹635 Cr Series C for compressed biogas

AI & Infrastructure (India)

Regional Tech Policy & Regulation

India's IT Amendment Rules 2026 in force; AI-content labelling obligations

India's amended IT (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules 2026 — notified February 10 and enforced from February 20 — bring "synthetically generated information" (AI text, image, audio, video) into intermediary due-diligence obligations, targeting deepfakes and AI-enabled harms. IT Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw has signalled the need for a dedicated AI law. (Context; rules effective 2026; flagged for ongoing relevance)
- Chambers and Partners — IT Amendment Rules 2026

Key Takeaways

  • AI-agent security is the live battleground. Three of June 13's notable cybersecurity disclosures — Agentjacking, the LangGraph chain, and AI-assisted BugHunter — all sit at the intersection of AI tooling and offensive/defensive security. Teams shipping AI coding agents should treat the agent runtime as an attack surface.
  • Indian deep-tech funding is broadening, not just scaling. The week's roundup (~$253M, up ~53% w/w) spread across clean energy (GPS Renewables), precision manufacturing (Ethereal Machines), consumer AI (Equal AI) and health fintech (QubeHealth-Pay) — a healthier mix than late-stage megadeals.
  • Infrastructure remains the throughline. Meta–Reliance's Jamnagar lease underscores that hyperscaler AI capacity continues to anchor in India on the back of cheap power and long tax holidays.
  • Policy is catching up to synthetic media. India's enforced IT Amendment Rules and signals toward a dedicated AI law mean compliance obligations around AI-generated content are now operational, not theoretical.
