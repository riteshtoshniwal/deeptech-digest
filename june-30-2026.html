AI & Machine Learning

South Korea unveils $576 billion AI-chip mega-project with Samsung and SK Hynix

South Korea rolled out sweeping chip and AI mega-projects on Monday, June 29, with President Lee Jae Myung pledging more than $576 billion in investment over several years to cement "overwhelming" industry leadership. Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix said they will invest a combined 800 trillion won (about $518 billion) to build a new chipmaking hub in the country's southwest, each constructing two fabrication plants. Separately, SK Group, GS Group and Naver committed 550 trillion won to AI data centres targeting 8.4 GW of capacity initially, scaling to 18.4 GW by 2035. The move is the most aggressive sovereign-compute play in Asia this year and raises the competitive bar for India and Southeast Asia's own AI-infrastructure ambitions. (Published: 2026-06-29)

India's sovereign-AI war chest and a two-track AI jobs market

A June 29 industry digest highlighted how India's foundation-model funding has reached a new high, anchored by Sarvam's $234M first close (led by a $150M strategic check from HCLTech) for a model trained, hosted and governed inside India and aimed at coding, agentic and cybersecurity workloads. The same brief flagged fresh PwC labour data showing AI is splitting the job market into two tracks: "professionalised" roles where AI handles routine work and humans bring judgement are growing twice as fast, with pay rising 42% faster and the AI-skills wage premium now at 62% (up from 57% a year earlier). For Indian engineering hiring, this concentrates senior ML and infra talent in a few well-funded labs and pushes compensation up. (Published: 2026-06-29)

Cybersecurity

Jaguar Land Rover breach shows hallmarks of Kremlin-backed hackers, experts warn

On June 29, security researchers warned that the Jaguar Land Rover breach bears the hallmarks of Kremlin-backed actors, pointing to novel ransomware, strategic timing and deliberate efforts to obscure attribution. The assessment adds the incident to a growing list of 2026 attacks on manufacturing and automotive supply chains, a sector that has become a priority target across Asia and Europe alike. (Published: 2026-06-29)
- The Hacker News
- SecurityWeek

China-linked group targets Southeast Asian critical infrastructure with new "TinyRCT" backdoor

Threat intelligence circulating around June 29 detailed a China-linked threat group deploying a new custom backdoor dubbed "TinyRCT" against critical infrastructure in Southeast Asia, continuing a pattern of state-aligned espionage focused on government and utility environments across the region. The activity underscores how SEA critical infrastructure remains a heavily contested target as geopolitical tensions spill into cyberspace. (Published: 2026-06-29)
- The Hacker News

CISA flags actively exploited Oracle E-Business Suite and LiteSpeed cPanel flaws

Defenders were tracking active exploitation of a critical Oracle E-Business Suite vulnerability (CVE-2026-46817) in financial applications, while CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog carried a June 29 remediation due date for a UNIX symlink-following flaw in the LiteSpeed cPanel plugin. Enterprises running these widely deployed enterprise and hosting stacks — common across Indian and SEA IT estates — were urged to patch immediately. (Published: 2026-06-29)

Southeast Asia — Regional Tech & Infrastructure

ASEAN's data-centre investment race nears US$30 billion

Regional coverage on June 29 underscored ASEAN's accelerating race to attract data-centre investment, with regional spending nearing US$30 billion and demand projected to grow roughly 20% annually through 2028. Singapore remains the premium hub (steering AI as a national capability under NAIS 2.0), while Vietnam, Malaysia and Indonesia compete on cost and incentives to absorb AI and overflow workloads. The build-out is the physical backbone for Southeast Asia's shift from AI experimentation to enterprise deployment. (Published: 2026-06-29)

Key Takeaways

  • Sovereign compute is the defining theme. South Korea's $576B chip-and-AI push (June 29) and India's HCLTech-backed Sarvam model both point to nations and national champions racing to own — not rent — their AI stacks. Expect rising senior AI-talent costs across India in particular.
  • The AI jobs market is bifurcating. Fresh PwC data cited June 29 shows "judgement" roles pulling away from "anyone-can-do-it" roles on both growth and pay; entry-level specs are quietly being rewritten around senior skills.
  • Cybersecurity pressure stays acute in Asia. A China-linked "TinyRCT" campaign against SEA critical infrastructure, actively exploited enterprise flaws (Oracle EBS, LiteSpeed cPanel), and attribution debates around the Jaguar Land Rover breach all surfaced June 29. (Context: India's Tata Electronics breach — 630GB allegedly tied to Apple/Tesla — remained an active story this week, first confirmed ~June 23.)
  • Southeast Asia's data-centre boom is the regional headline. ASEAN spending nearing US$30B is the infrastructure layer enabling the region's enterprise-AI transition, with Singapore leading and Vietnam/Indonesia rising on cost.
  • Policy watch (context). India's MeitY signalled a "decisive phase" in its semiconductor and AI push (12 projects, 45,000+ GPUs) around June 27, just ahead of this window — a backdrop to the sovereign-AI momentum above.
