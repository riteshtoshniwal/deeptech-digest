Cybersecurity

New GoSerpent malware targets Southeast Asian governments and diplomats

Kaspersky researchers disclosed GoSerpent, a previously undocumented backdoor targeting government and diplomatic entities in Southeast Asia since late 2025, focused on long-term access and intelligence gathering. The malware deliberately waits weeks before deploying secondary exfiltration tools like TmcLoader to outlast log retention policies, and shares overlaps with the TetrisPhantom threat actor. (Published: 2026-07-17)

Kudankulam nuclear plant data leak raises supply-chain security alarms

Analysis published on July 17 flagged the leak of 14.3 GB (nearly 19,000 files) of Kudankulam Nuclear Power Plant-related data — including blueprints of ventilation and cooling systems for Units 3 and 4 — posted on the dark web by ransomware group World Leaks, sourced from a partial breach of Reliance Group data on a third-party server. NPCIL said no sensitive nuclear-security information was revealed, but experts warn the files could help attackers map support systems and suppliers. (Reported: 2026-07-17)

"AI Squatting" flagged as an emerging threat class

Security researchers warned about AI Squatting, where attackers weaponise predictable AI hallucinations: large language models invent convincing but non-existent websites or software packages, and attackers register those fake assets to steal credentials, harvest data, or distribute malware. The threat is particularly relevant for India's fast-growing developer ecosystem relying on AI coding assistants. (Reported: 2026-07-17)
- Insights on India — Current Affairs, 17 July 2026

Key Takeaways

  1. Enterprise AI is moving from pilots to plumbing in India. Myntra, HCLTech, LTM–Glean, and Zithara.AI announcements on the same day all centre on operationalising AI in core workflows — commerce operations, financial services delivery, enterprise knowledge, and retail CRM — rather than experimentation.
  2. Southeast Asia remains a prime target for patient, state-aligned cyber-espionage. GoSerpent's weeks-long dwell time before exfiltration is designed to defeat standard log retention — a wake-up call for government and diplomatic networks in the region to extend detection windows.
  3. Third-party and supply-chain risk is India's critical-infrastructure weak point. The Kudankulam leak originated not from the plant but from a vendor's data on a third-party hosted server — reinforcing the case for supplier-level security mandates around critical infrastructure.
  4. Indian deeptech milestones are compounding. ideaForge's 1 million UAV flights and Raptee.HV's high-voltage EV retail expansion show maturing indigenous hardware platforms, while funding continues to flow to manufacturing-adjacent plays (Naturis' Rs 100 Cr round).
  5. AI-era threat surfaces are widening. "AI Squatting" — weaponising LLM hallucinations — signals that AI adoption itself is becoming an attack vector, relevant to every developer team using AI coding assistants.
