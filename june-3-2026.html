🔐 Cybersecurity

CERT-In Mandates 12-Hour Fix for Critical Cyber Flaws as AI Threats Surge

India's national cybersecurity agency CERT-In issued updated guidelines mandating that organizations patch high-risk security flaws within 12 hours wherever feasible, reflecting the shrinking vulnerability-to-exploit timelines as threat actors increasingly use AI and automation. The new framework also outlines defensive principles including zero-trust architecture, defence-in-depth strategies, and secure-by-design development. A dedicated CERT-In AI Cyber Defence Center has been announced as a new unit for AI cybersecurity compliance. (Published: 2026-06-02)

A new Business Standard analysis published on June 2, 2026 highlights how AI is compressing the time it takes for attackers to discover and exploit software vulnerabilities, with bots now accounting for roughly 53% of internet traffic (Thales Bad Bot Report 2026) — nearly 40% classified as bad or unverified. India's AI Regulation Framework Tightens: MeitY Targets Synthetic Content

India's Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY) amendments effective February 2026 now specifically target "synthetically generated information" (SGI) — AI-generated or AI-altered content that appears authentic — with new compliance requirements for platforms operating in India. Global firms are facing new legal risks under India's 2026 AI regulation framework, which places compliance obligations on both domestic platforms and foreign operators serving Indian users. The regulatory tightening mirrors India's broader push to balance AI innovation with consumer protection. (Published: 2026-06-02)
