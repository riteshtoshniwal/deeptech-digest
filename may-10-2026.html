🤖 ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

AI Disruption Pushing 1 in 3 BFSI Employees Towards Exit

Rapid AI adoption, rising work pressure, and fears around changing job roles are pushing India's Banking, Financial Services and Insurance (BFSI) sector workforce to the brink. A new report by Great Place To Work India reveals that nearly one in three BFSI employees are now considering quitting their jobs. Nearly half of the entire BFSI workforce requires reskilling to stay relevant, yet only 7% of employees currently engage in proactive learning. Managers and frontline staff are identified as the most stressed groups, with a 42% AI skills gap reported across the sector. (Published: 2026-05-09)

India's Market Underperformance Driven by 'AI Exposure Gap', Not Domestic Weakness — Motilal Oswal

Motilal Oswal Financial Services released its May 2026 strategy report "The Eagle Eye," arguing that India's stock market underperformance versus global peers is due to limited exposure to the global AI hardware rally — not underlying domestic weakness. Markets like South Korea (+53% YTD in USD terms), Taiwan (+33%), and the US have surged on AI semiconductor and hardware strength, while India's Nifty declined ~13% in USD terms. The report notes that DIIs injected USD 5.4 billion for the 12th consecutive month of over $5B inflows, and that if the global AI trade unwinds, capital could rotate back to growth economies like India. (Published: 2026-05-09)

  • Tribune India — Same story with additional context

Google Gemini Expands in Chrome to Asia-Pacific Users

Google announced on May 9 that Chrome's latest Gemini AI features are rolling out to desktop and iOS users across key Asia-Pacific markets, including Indonesia, Philippines, Singapore, and Vietnam. This marks a significant expansion of Google's AI browser integration into Southeast Asia, bringing features such as AI-assisted browsing and summarization to millions of new users in the region. (Published: 2026-05-09)

🛡️ CYBERSECURITY

Hacktivist DDoS Attacks Against India Decline Sharply; Pakistan Groups Still Active

Radware shared new analysis with Dark Reading on May 9 showing that hacktivist cyberattack activity targeting India has fallen precipitously each day since May 7, following a surge linked to the India-Pakistan conflict. Researchers from NSFOCUS had tracked a 500% rise in attacks against India and a 700% spike against Pakistan. CyberKnow has been monitoring 45 hacktivist groups (10 from India, 35 from Pakistan), predominantly executing DDoS attacks and website defacements — 95% of which had no real operational impact. India proactively blocked international access to the National Stock Exchange (NSE) and Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE) as a defensive measure, though Pascal Geenens of Radware notes serious attacks have not materialized. Separately, SecurityWeek reported on the broader India-Pakistan cyberattack landscape alongside Radware vulnerabilities and an xAI data leak. (Published: 2026-05-09)

No Ceasefire in Cyberspace: India-Pakistan Digital Conflict Continues

Despite a fragile military ceasefire, The Cyber Express reported that cyber operations between India- and Pakistan-aligned groups continue with no let-up. Pakistani hackers, including Pakistan Cyber Force, have claimed access to sensitive data from India's Military Engineering Service and the Manohar Parrikar Institute of Defence Studies and Analysis. CERT-In issued alerts, and BSE notified market participants to stay vigilant. CYFIRMA and CloudSEK published independent analyses confirming the persistence of cyber operations even as kinetic activity paused. (Published: 2026-05-09)

🚀 DEEP TECH & STARTUPS

Southeast Asia Deep Tech Startups Eye Japan for Growth Capital

A KR-Asia analysis highlights how Southeast Asian deep tech startups — typically spun out of university research in semiconductors, robotics, and pharma — are increasingly looking to Japan for patient capital and market partnerships. Singapore-based Accelerated Materials (backed by SGInnovate and Cambridge Enterprise) uses AI to accelerate nanoparticle discovery for the semiconductor and pharmaceutical industries. Vietnam-based Alterno, backed by ADB Ventures, is developing sand-based thermal batteries for renewable energy storage and is targeting a Japanese public listing within five years. The pivot to Japan reflects a growing gap between what deep tech startups need (long-horizon capital) and what regional VCs typically offer. (Published: 2026-05-09, ongoing coverage)

🌏 REGIONAL TECH POLICY

Southeast Asia's AI Governance Frameworks Lag Behind AI Adoption

TechWire Asia reports that while Southeast Asia leads globally in AI optimism — with more than 80% of respondents in Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia, and Singapore believing AI will profoundly change their lives in the next three to five years — regulatory frameworks remain dangerously underdeveloped. Vietnam has implemented Southeast Asia's first comprehensive AI law (modeled on the EU AI Act), while Thailand's "Cloud First" policy has diverted 500 billion THB in investment toward domestic infrastructure. Malaysia's National AI Office now mandates that sensitive government data remains on local soil. The Diplomat calls this the "Southeast Asia AI Dilemma": massive optimism with minimal governance infrastructure. (Published: 2026-05-09, ongoing coverage)

India Has a Chance to Lead the World on Ethical AI

A new analysis published on May 8 in Business Standard argues that India is emerging as one of the most consequential testing grounds for ethical AI deployment — not just regulation. With 91 countries having signed the India AI Impact Summit Declaration in February 2026 and the government allocating ₹10,000 crore (potentially doubling to ₹20,000 crore) to the IndiaAI Mission, the country's sheer scale and diversity make it uniquely positioned to demonstrate responsible, inclusive AI adoption at a global level. (Published: 2026-05-08)

📊 KEY TAKEAWAYS

  1. Cybersecurity remains India's top operational concern — The India-Pakistan hacktivist surge underscores how geopolitical conflicts now have an immediate cyber dimension; 45 groups, mostly Pakistani, are active but largely ineffective (95% no real impact). CERT-In and stock exchange alerts remain in force.

  2. AI is reshaping India's financial sector workforce — 1 in 3 BFSI employees considering quitting due to AI-driven stress is a leading indicator of wider disruption. The 42% AI skills gap points to an urgent reskilling imperative for India's largest employment sectors.

  3. India's AI exposure gap is a double-edged sword — Underperformance vs. AI-heavy markets like South Korea and Taiwan today; potential re-rating opportunity if the AI hardware trade unwinds, per Motilal Oswal.

  4. Southeast Asia governance is racing to catch up with AI adoption — Vietnam leads with an EU-style AI law; others (Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia) are implementing sovereign data and compute policies, but comprehensive frameworks lag adoption curves by years.

  5. Deep tech capital gap in Southeast Asia — Japan is becoming a critical partner for regional deep tech startups that need long-horizon capital, as local VCs remain oriented toward faster-returning consumer tech plays.

  6. Google's Gemini Chrome expansion into APAC (Indonesia, Philippines, Singapore, Vietnam) signals that AI browser integration is becoming a mainstream battleground in the region.
