📋 Tech Policy & Regulations

India's IT Rules 2026: AI Content Must Be Labeled; Takedown Windows Tighten

India's amended IT (Intermediary Guidelines) Rules 2026 now require all platforms and users to declare and label AI-generated content. Platforms must remove non-consensual intimate imagery within 2 hours and other unlawful AI-generated content within 3 hours (down from 36 hours). The rules also introduce stricter compliance obligations for social media intermediaries regarding deepfakes and misinformation.

Maharashtra Approves AI Policy 2026: ₹10,000 Crore Investment Target

The Maharashtra Cabinet approved its Artificial Intelligence Policy 2026, targeting ₹10,000 crore in AI investments and 1.5 lakh new jobs by 2031. The policy prioritises building datasets in Marathi, regional dialects, and tribal languages, and establishes a "State AI Data Exchange" linked with the central IndiaAI Mission's infrastructure.

India Doubles Deeptech Startup Classification Period to 20 Years

In February 2026, the Indian government updated its startup framework to double the period during which deep tech companies qualify as startups to 20 years (up from 10), and raised the revenue threshold for startup-specific tax, grant, and regulatory benefits to ₹3 billion (~$33M). This change is designed to support the longer commercialisation timelines inherent in deep tech ventures.

Singapore Launches Model AI Governance Framework for Agentic AI

Singapore's IMDA released a new Model AI Governance Framework specifically for agentic AI — the first of its kind in the region — offering structured guidance on risks and best practices for autonomous AI agents acting across systems and platforms. Singapore continues to co-lead the ASEAN Working Group on AI Governance (WG-AI), promoting a consistent regional baseline while ASEAN's broader framework remains voluntary and non-binding.

📌 Key Takeaways

  1. India's sovereign AI ambition is gaining traction — from LLMs to AI wearables, Indian startups backed by the IndiaAI Mission are building end-to-end AI stacks to reduce dependence on Western platforms.

  2. Defence-tech is the hottest deeptech vertical in India right now — Olee Space's DEW delivery and Apollyon Dynamics' pre-seed round signal that private-sector defence and UAV tech is moving from prototypes to deployment.

  3. Southeast Asia remains a split story — Singapore consolidates its dominance as a deep tech hub while Indonesia and other markets struggle to attract capital, creating an uneven regional innovation landscape.

  4. AI-powered threats are escalating across the region — from cPanel exploits targeting SEA governments to Silver Fox tax-themed attacks in India, threat actors are becoming more targeted and sophisticated.

  5. Regulatory frameworks are hardening — India's IT Rules 2026, Maharashtra's AI Policy, and Singapore's Agentic AI Governance Framework all signal a shift from voluntary guidelines toward enforceable standards, with implications for global tech platforms operating in the region.
