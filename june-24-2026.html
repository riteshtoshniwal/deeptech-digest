AI & Machine Learning

OpenAI expands "Daybreak" with GPT-5.5-Cyber to help defenders patch security flaws

OpenAI released an improved GPT-5.5-Cyber model to trusted defenders as part of its Daybreak initiative, calling it its "strongest model yet for finding and helping patch software vulnerabilities." The model can sustain deeper analysis across large codebases to identify issues, validate them in a controlled environment, and develop and test patches. OpenAI also shipped an update to its Codex Security plugin to speed up vulnerability discovery and remediation. This is directly relevant to India and SEA enterprises and SOC teams adopting AI-assisted defensive tooling. (Published: 2026-06-23)
- The Hacker News

Fake AI agent "skill" passed every security scanner and reportedly reached 26,000 agents

Security firm AIR built a benign-but-fake AI agent skill, pushed it through a popular skill marketplace and an Instagram ad, and says it reached roughly 26,000 agents — including some on corporate accounts. Every skill-security scanner tested marked it safe, and GitHub stars and open-source reputation failed as trust signals. The exercise highlights a growing supply-chain risk as agentic AI adoption accelerates across enterprises in India and SEA. (Published: 2026-06-23)
- The Hacker News

Op-ed: "Agentic AI — the weapon that no longer needs a warrior"

A widely-shared analysis on offensive AI argued that agentic systems mark the point at which the cyber "weapon learns to aim itself," moving beyond AI as a drafting assistant for phishing or exploit code toward autonomous targeting. The piece frames the strategic shift defenders and policymakers must prepare for as AI agents gain operational autonomy. (Published: 2026-06-23)
- The Hacker News

Cybersecurity Threats & Solutions

WhatsApp VBScript campaign targets India and Southeast Asia with fake documents

Kaspersky disclosed an active campaign distributing malicious VBScript files via WhatsApp Desktop and Web that ultimately install legitimate ManageEngine RMM software for remote access. Victims are concentrated across Malaysia, Brazil, India, Mexico, Singapore, Spain, Taiwan, Australia, Russia and Vietnam, with the highest concentration in Malaysia. Lures masquerade as business and financial documents — a direct threat to users and SMEs across the India-SEA corridor. (Published: 2026-06-23)
- The Hacker News

"FortiBleed" harvested credentials from 430,000+ FortiGate firewalls

A Russian-speaking, financially-motivated initial access broker is assessed to be behind FortiBleed, a large-scale credential-harvesting operation active since February 2026 that has targeted over 430,000 FortiGate firewalls globally. The actor deploys a Golang sniffer (FortigateSniffer) abusing FortiOS's built-in diagnostic packet command to passively capture authentication traffic, then cracks and reuses credentials against Active Directory and exposed services. Given heavy Fortinet deployment across Indian and SEA enterprises, exposure review is warranted. (Published: 2026-06-23)
- The Hacker News

Malicious npm packages pose as PostCSS tools to deliver a Windows RAT

Researchers found a set of malicious npm packages (aes-decode-runner-pro, postcss-minify-selector, postcss-minify-selector-parser) published by a user named "abdrizak" that masquerade as PostCSS tooling while delivering a Windows remote access trojan. The packages remained downloadable at the time of reporting — a reminder of ongoing developer-supply-chain risk for the region's large engineering workforce. (Published: 2026-06-23)
- The Hacker News

GitHub updates actions/checkout to block "pwn request" attacks by default

GitHub hardened software-supply-chain security: the latest actions/checkout now refuses common pwn-request patterns that abuse the pull_request_target trigger to run untrusted fork code with full workflow privileges. Effective June 18, 2026, the change will be backported to all supported major versions on July 16, 2026. (Published: 2026-06-23)
- The Hacker News

Tech Policy & Regulation

U.S. executive order sets a 2030 deadline for federal post-quantum crypto migration

An executive order signed June 22 sets hard deadlines for U.S. federal agencies to move high-value assets to post-quantum cryptography: key establishment by December 31, 2030 and digital signatures by December 31, 2031, pulling the prior 2035 target forward by several years. The deadlines align with NIST's finalized standards (FIPS 203 / ML-KEM). While U.S.-specific, it sets a benchmark that Indian and SEA governments and enterprises tracking "harvest now, decrypt later" risk are likely to follow. (Published: 2026-06-23)
- The Hacker News
