Artificial Intelligence

US assures India AI access won't be "cut off" as "Pax Silica" expands

On the sidelines of the US-led "Pax Silica" summit in Washington — an initiative to build China-free AI supply chains — S. Krishnan, secretary of India's Ministry of Electronics and IT, raised concerns over an AI "kill switch" days after Washington abruptly restricted access to certain advanced models on national-security grounds. US officials reportedly assured the Indian delegation that "access to technology, once it is provided, will not be cut off." (Published: 2026-06-26)
- South China Morning Post

Nasscom US CEO Forum: India's tech-services sector to stay central in the AI era

At the Nasscom US CEO Forum, industry leaders argued India's technology-services sector will remain central to global enterprise transformation in the AI era. Agentic AI is projected to open an additional US$300–400 billion addressable spend pool for tech services by 2030 across data-for-AI, legacy modernisation, agentic workflows, AI operations, cybersecurity and AI governance. (Published: 2026-06-26)
- PR Newswire

Cybersecurity

AI-driven cyber threats reshape enterprise cybersecurity spending in India

Indian enterprises are restructuring security budgets as AI-enabled threats — including AI-driven phishing and identity attacks — accelerate. Reporting cited CERT-In guidance urging faster patch management, continuous monitoring of internet-facing systems, stronger vulnerability management, Zero Trust architecture, multi-factor authentication, network segmentation and improved incident-response readiness as frontier-AI systems amplify attacker capabilities. (Published: 2026-06-26)
- Business Standard

Key Takeaways

  • AI geopolitics front and centre: India is actively negotiating guaranteed, non-revocable access to advanced AI models within the US-led "Pax Silica" bloc — a signal that supply-chain sovereignty is now a core policy concern, not just an industry one.
  • Cybersecurity budgets follow the threat curve: Indian enterprises are re-allocating spend toward Zero Trust, identity and AI-governance controls as attackers operationalise frontier AI.
  • Services thesis intact: Nasscom's US$300–400B agentic-AI spend-pool projection reframes AI as an expansion driver for Indian IT services rather than a displacement risk.
  • Deal momentum holds: Early-stage deep-tech and AI funding continues globally, consistent with H1 2026 trends where AI and climate-infrastructure were the strongest investor themes.
