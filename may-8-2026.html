India's homegrown sovereign AI company Sarvam AI unveiled two large language models — Sarvam-30B and Sarvam-105B — at the India AI Impact Summit 2026. 🔐 CYBERSECURITY

India Faces 2,011 Cyberattacks Per Week — WEF Ranks It Top National Risk

The World Economic Forum Global Risk Report 2026 ranks cybersecurity as India's number one national risk, above economic downturns and climate disasters. Check Point's "State of Cyber Security in India 2025" report documented an average of 2,011 cyberattacks per week against Indian organisations — far above the global average. Between 2021 and mid-2025, India recorded over 2.2 million cybersecurity incidents.

Check Point Research documented "Operation TrueChaos," a zero-day exploitation campaign targeting Southeast Asian government networks attributed to a Chinese-linked threat actor. The INTERPOL Asia and South Pacific Cyber Threat Assessment Report 2025/2026 also highlighted an alarming rise in AI-enabled deepfake scams and industrial-scale fraud operations across the region.

IDC forecasts total cybersecurity spending across Asia-Pacific will reach $39.5 billion in 2026, growing at a 10% CAGR to $52.4 billion by 2029. AI-driven detection and response tools are seeing surging adoption, especially as SMEs — over 80% of which operate without full-time security staff — become primary targets for ransomware and BEC attacks.

📋 TECH POLICY & REGULATION

India's IT Rules 2026: Mandatory AI Content Labels and 3-Hour Deepfake Takedowns

India's amended IT Rules, notified February 10, 2026, require all platforms and users to declare and label AI-generated content with permanent provenance metadata. Critically, the window for taking down deepfake or synthetic content has been tightened from 36 hours to just 3 hours. Global firms face new legal risks for non-compliance under the framework effective February 20, 2026.

  • India Briefing — AI Regulation Compliance

DPIIT Extends Deeptech Startup Recognition Period to 20 Years

The Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT) formally recognised deeptech startups as a distinct category in India's startup policy framework (February 4, 2026). Key changes include extending the recognition period to 20 years (vs 10 for regular startups), raising the turnover threshold to ₹300 crore, and acknowledging the unique high-R&D, long-gestation nature of deeptech ventures.

  • NASSCOM — DPIIT Deeptech Startup Definition
  • TechCrunch — India Changes Startup Rules for Deeptech

Malaysia Restricts New Data Centres to AI Projects Only

Malaysia's PM Anwar Ibrahim confirmed in February 2026 that new data centre developments will be restricted exclusively to AI-related projects. The country has also allocated approximately $490 million in its 2026 budget for a sovereign AI cloud. Malaysia, Singapore, Vietnam, Indonesia, and Thailand are all pursuing "AI Sovereignty" as a strategic national priority.

💡 KEY TAKEAWAYS

  1. India's deeptech moment is real — From laser weapons to orbital AI data centres, Indian startups are moving from services to deep science. The DPIIT's formal recognition of deeptech as a distinct startup category (with 20-year recognition windows) gives the ecosystem a long-runway policy anchor.

  2. Cybersecurity is India's #1 national risk — With 2,011 attacks per week on average, cloud misconfigurations, ransomware, and AI-enabled fraud are the dominant threat vectors. Enterprises and SMEs alike must treat security as a board-level priority.

  3. SEA AI governance lags the technology — While Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, and Indonesia are racing toward AI sovereignty with data centre investments and workforce training, policy frameworks are struggling to keep up with fast-moving AI deployment, especially in cybersecurity.

  4. Funding is selective, not scarce — Both India and SEA are seeing investors concentrate capital in AI and deeptech while pulling back from consumer plays. The Q1 2026 deal count in SEA hitting an 8-year low reflects quality-over-quantity filtering, not a capital drought.

  5. Japan is emerging as a bridge for SEA deeptech — As domestic SEA markets remain small and VC funding stays thin, Japanese corporations and investors are becoming key growth partners for science-driven SEA startups seeking scale.
