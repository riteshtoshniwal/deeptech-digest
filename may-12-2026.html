🔐 Cybersecurity

CloudSEK Debunks India-Pakistan Hacktivist Claims — Real APT36 Threat Underscored

Cybersecurity firm CloudSEK published an analysis on May 11 revealing that most of the 100+ cyberattack claims made by Pakistan-aligned hacktivist groups during and after Operation Sindoor were exaggerated or outright false. Groups like SYLHET GANG-SG claimed to have stolen 247GB from India's National Informatics Centre, but investigators found only publicly available marketing materials in a 1.5GB sample. Meanwhile, the real and sophisticated threat comes from APT36 (Transparent Tribe), a Pakistan-linked espionage group actively deploying Crimson RAT — a .NET-based Remote Access Trojan — against Indian government and defence networks. (Published: 2026-05-11)

India-Pakistan Cyberattacks, Radware Vulnerabilities, xAI API Key Leak — SecurityWeek Roundup

SecurityWeek's weekly roundup highlighted several notable cybersecurity developments. Pakistan-aligned hackers launched a campaign to exfiltrate data from Indian government, academic, and strategic institutions. Separately, CERT/CC disclosed that Radware's Cloud WAF could be bypassed using specially crafted HTTP requests — vulnerabilities now patched. In another incident, an xAI employee inadvertently leaked an API key on GitHub, which was accessible for roughly two months and could have been used to query private LLMs built for Tesla, SpaceX, and X. (Published: 2026-05-11)

🤖 AI & Machine Learning

Asia Emerges as New Benchmark in AI Ethics and Governance

A detailed analysis published by TNGlobal on May 11 argues that Asia is not merely adapting to global AI standards — it is actively reshaping them. Countries across the region are embedding ethics directly into AI system design rather than treating governance as an external compliance layer. Vietnam's landmark AI Law (effective March 2026), Singapore's testing-and-assurance leadership, and ASEAN's evolving soft-to-hard law transition are all cited as evidence of a structural shift. The piece notes that Asia's approach prioritises context-awareness and scale, making it a model for large, diverse human systems globally. (Published: 2026-05-11)

💰 Deep Tech Startups & Investment

BigEndian Semiconductors Raises $6M to Advance India's Fabless Chip Ecosystem

Bengaluru-based fabless chip startup BigEndian Semiconductors closed a $6 million pre-Series A funding round on May 11, led by IAN Alpha Fund with participation from Vertex Ventures SEA & India, IvyCap Ventures, and strategic angels. The capital will be used to commercialise BigEndian's first system-on-chip (SoC) — already successfully taped out — targeting surveillance, telecom, IoT, and enterprise markets. The round follows a $3M seed from Vertex Ventures in 2024 and signals continued investor confidence in India's push for indigenous semiconductor design capability. (Published: 2026-05-11)

India Deep Tech Alliance Selects 20 IIT-Affiliated Startups for Inaugural Cohort

The University of Chicago's Polsky Center announced the 20 Indian startups selected for the inaugural cohort of the India Deep Tech Alliance (IDTA) Accelerator, with many reports covering the announcement on May 11. The 10-week programme (April–June 2026) gives IIT-linked deep tech companies access to global customers, partners, and investors. Cohort members span AI, robotics, semiconductors, space technology, advanced materials, healthtech, and climate-energy sectors. Notable participants include Adaapt (AI middleware), circuitEvolve (AI-assisted chip design), Cerify Auditing Solutions (smart contract security AI), and Augle.AI (edge AI robotics). (Published: 2026-05-11)

🚀 Notable Tech Industry Developments

Skyroot Aerospace Becomes India's First Space-Tech Unicorn with $60M Round

Hyderabad-based Skyroot Aerospace crossed the unicorn threshold after closing a $60 million funding round, reaching a valuation of $1.1 billion — making it India's first space-tech unicorn. The round was co-led by Singapore's sovereign wealth fund GIC and Sherpalo Ventures (founded by Ram Shriram, an early Google backer and Alphabet board member, who will join Skyroot's board). Other investors include BlackRock funds, Greenko Group founders, Arkam Ventures, and Playbook Partners. The capital will accelerate Vikram-1 launch cadence, expand manufacturing, and advance development of the orbital-class Vikram-2 rocket. (Published: 2026-05-07 to 2026-05-10)

📋 Key Takeaways & Insights

  1. Cyber operations outlast kinetic conflict: CloudSEK's May 11 report confirms that while India-Pakistan military operations wound down, hacktivist cyber activity continued — a pattern CYFIRMA calls a "feedback loop" between battlefield events and online skirmishes. The bigger concern is sophisticated APT36 espionage, not headline-grabbing DDoS attacks.

  2. India's semiconductor ambitions gain momentum: BigEndian's $6M raise — backed by both Indian (IAN Alpha) and Southeast Asian (Vertex Ventures SEA) capital — reflects the cross-regional investor thesis around India's chip design ecosystem, buoyed by government policy extending deep tech startup status to 20 years.

  3. Asia rewrites AI governance norms: The TNGlobal analysis on May 11 marks a maturing narrative: Asia isn't playing catch-up with the EU AI Act — it's building its own governance model centered on embedded ethics, risk-based oversight, and scale for heterogeneous populations. Vietnam, Singapore, and ASEAN are the leading examples.

  4. India's space-tech breakout continues: Skyroot's unicorn milestone signals that India's private space sector — catalysed by ISRO reforms and global institutional capital (GIC, BlackRock) — is producing globally competitive ventures. The orbital-class Vikram-2 development will be the next key milestone to watch.

  5. Deep tech accelerators bridge lab to market: The IDTA cohort's selection of IIT-affiliated startups in AI, chips, and climate-tech reflects a broader shift: Indian deeptech is transitioning from research output to commercially deployable products, supported by global accelerator infrastructure.
