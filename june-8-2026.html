Cybersecurity

Verizon 2026 DBIR: Vulnerability Exploitation Overtakes Stolen Credentials as Top Breach Vector

The 2026 Verizon Data Breach Investigations Report — arguably the most authoritative annual cybersecurity benchmark — confirmed a historic milestone: for the first time in 19 years of publication, exploiting vulnerabilities (31% of breaches) has overtaken stolen credentials as the number-one breach entry point. AI is being leveraged by threat actors to shrink the window from disclosure to exploit from months to mere hours. Only 26% of critical vulnerabilities in CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerability catalogue were fully remediated in 2025, down from 38% the prior year. Ransomware now touches 96% of SMBs. (Published: 2026-05-25; Key reference document for the week of June 1–7)

White House Signs Executive Order on AI Innovation and Security

The Trump administration signed a new AI executive order directing federal agencies to develop benchmarks for assessing AI models' cyber capabilities, create an "AI cybersecurity clearinghouse," and shore up government defenses against AI-powered threats. The order also includes voluntary review requirements for new AI model releases, putting the US on a formal track toward AI security governance — with implications for how India and SEA governments calibrate their own frameworks. (Published: 2026-06-07)

AI & Machine Learning

Alibaba Unveils Qwen 3.7-Max at Inaugural Singapore Conference — Directly Targets SEA Enterprise AI

Alibaba Cloud hosted the inaugural international Qwen Conference at Marina Bay Sands, Singapore, unveiling Qwen 3.7-Max, a proprietary model with over one trillion parameters and a one-million-token context window. The model claims to sustain autonomous agentic task execution for up to 35 hours. Alibaba also launched Qwen Cloud, an AI-native platform for human and AI agent use, alongside a new MCP-compatible skills portal spanning 60+ cloud products. A partnership with NTUC Tech Talent Assembly will upskill 1,000+ Singapore SMEs and students from June 2026. (Published: 2026-05-26; Widely covered in SEA tech press through week of June 7)

India's Sovereign AI Push Reshaping Asian Tech Ecosystem

India's AI infrastructure buildout — anchored in the IndiaAI Mission's subsidised GPU access at ₹65/hr and bilateral deals like the India-UAE G42/Core42 AI supercomputer — is being watched regionally as a "scale template" rather than a threat to Singapore and Malaysia. Key Takeaways

June 7 was a low-volume Sunday, but the week's signal is clear: AI is on both offense and defense in cybersecurity simultaneously. The Verizon DBIR confirms AI is shrinking exploit windows; the White House executive order is the first formal governance response in the US. India and Southeast Asia will be calibrating their own frameworks against this backdrop.

Alibaba is making a direct play for SEA enterprise AI through Singapore. Qwen Cloud + MCP integration + NTUC partnership is a packaged enterprise AI stack that competes directly with Microsoft, Google, and AWS in the region. Worth watching for Indian enterprise adoption signals.

India's sovereign AI narrative is becoming a regional export. Rather than creating zero-sum competition with Singapore and Malaysia, India's scale is providing a cost-efficient testing ground whose infrastructure surplus is flowing back into SEA — a dynamic that benefits portfolio companies building for cross-India/SEA deployment.

Sea Group entering active AI investment mode is a significant regional signal. Shopee and SeaMoney together touch hundreds of millions of users across SEA; their AI infrastructure choices will shape vendor selection across commerce, payments, and logistics for the next several years.
