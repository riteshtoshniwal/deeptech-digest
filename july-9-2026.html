AI & Machine Learning

PM Modi Inaugurates CG Semi OSAT Facility in Sanand, Backs Made-in-India Chips for AI and Robotics

Prime Minister Narendra Modi inaugurated the Rs 7,600-crore CG Semi Outsourced Semiconductor Assembly and Test (OSAT) facility in Sanand, Gujarat — a milestone for the India Semiconductor Mission. The plant, built with Renesas Electronics (US) and Stars Microelectronics (Thailand), has begun commercial chip packaging and is expected to produce around 20 crore semiconductor units annually, with Sanand targeting 1.5 crore chips per day. Modi said India's youth will drive the AI, robotics and next-generation technology revolution with Made-in-India chips. (Published: 2026-07-08)

Google Cloud CEO Thomas Kurian said the company intends to expand its AI data centre network in India as enterprise demand for artificial intelligence and cloud services continues to grow. The move would bolster Google's AI infrastructure footprint across the country, reinforcing India's position as a priority market for hyperscaler AI capacity. (Published: 2026-07-08)

Dream Sports CTO Amit Sharma Exits After a Decade to Build AI Startup

Amit Sharma, Chief Technology Officer of Dream Sports, resigned after nearly ten years to pursue his own AI venture, currently in stealth mode. Sharma, previously an engineer at Netflix and Yahoo, built Dream Sports' technology team from scratch. Cybersecurity

Kaspersky: AI Tools and Weak Passwords Driving Workplace Cyber Risk in Malaysia and SEA

Kaspersky reported that Malaysian businesses' use of AI tools, weak password habits, and hybrid work are compounding workplace cybersecurity risks. Globally, more than 92,000 malware and potentially-unwanted-application attacks disguised as AI services were detected between January and early May 2026, with fake ChatGPT accounting for nearly half. Southeast Asia logged over 1,800 SMB attacks involving malicious software disguised as AI services from January to April 2026 — nearly seven times higher year-on-year. (Published: 2026-07-08)
- Tech Wire Asia

China is reportedly considering limits on outside access to its most powerful AI models — a move that could affect developers and companies in India, the US and beyond. Officials are said to be weighing tougher controls on frontier AI technologies and related foreign investment, a development with direct implications for Indian firms building on Chinese open-weight models. Notable Industry Developments

Ola Electric Contests Insolvency Petitions from Two Suppliers; Shares Drop ~5%

Ola Electric is facing NCLT insolvency petitions from suppliers Anevolve Mando E-Mobility and Sterling E-Mobility Solutions over alleged unpaid dues. The company says the claims relate to pre-existing disputes already under arbitration and flagged warranty/performance concerns with parts from both vendors. Key Takeaways

  1. India's semiconductor stack is going live. The CG Semi OSAT inauguration moves India from announcements to commercial chip packaging, and the government is explicitly tying chips to its AI and robotics ambitions.
  2. Hyperscaler AI capacity keeps flowing into India. Google Cloud's plan for more AI data centres signals sustained enterprise AI demand — and intensifying infrastructure competition among hyperscalers in the region.
  3. AI is now the phishing lure of choice in SEA. Kaspersky's data showing a ~7x YoY jump in SEA SMB attacks disguised as AI services underlines how AI adoption is expanding the attack surface faster than security practices are maturing.
  4. Early-stage funding stays busy but small. Seven Indian rounds closed in a single day — mostly seed/pre-seed — consistent with the disciplined, early-stage-heavy funding climate of H1 2026.
  5. Geopolitics is entering the AI stack. Potential Chinese restrictions on frontier-model access would ripple through Indian developers who build on Chinese open-weight models, strengthening the case for sovereign AI capability.
