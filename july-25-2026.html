Cybersecurity

"Certighost" Exploit (CVE-2026-54121) Published — Low-Privileged Users Can Impersonate Domain Controllers

Researchers H0j3n and Aniq Fakhrul published a working exploit on July 24 for an Active Directory Certificate Services flaw dubbed Certighost (CVE-2026-54121, CVSS 8.8). The bug lets a low-privileged domain user obtain a certificate for a Domain Controller, authenticate as that machine, and retrieve the krbtgt secret via DCSync — enabling full domain takeover. Microsoft patched the flaw in its July 2026 security updates; enterprises across India and SEA running AD CS should verify the patch is applied. (Published: 2026-07-24)

Key Takeaways

  1. Enterprise AI is consolidating around infrastructure and governance. Genpact's agentic AML platform, the Microsoft–Databricks extension, and Progress–Domo all point the same direction: the enterprise AI battle has moved from models to governed data platforms and auditable agent workflows.

  2. The AI capex cycle keeps broadening across Asia. Intel's blowout results and Huawei's Thailand infrastructure launch show AI investment widening beyond GPUs into CPUs, packaging, and sovereign-cloud buildouts — with SEA (Thailand, Malaysia, Singapore) an active battleground.

  3. Patch AD CS now. The public Certighost exploit (CVE-2026-54121) turns any low-privileged domain account into a potential domain takeover. With the exploit code now public, expect rapid weaponization; the July 2026 Microsoft update closes it.

  4. India's deeptech capital flywheel is turning. A 20x PE exit from LOHUM, a $100M student-deeptech fund adding marquee advisors, and corporate-backed IIT Bombay innovation grants all landed on a single day — evidence of a maturing early-stage deeptech pipeline.
